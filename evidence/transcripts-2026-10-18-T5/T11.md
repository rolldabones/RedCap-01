## 6. Recommendations (future state)

Each recommendation states how it improves decision quality.

**R1. Make risk input a gate for sourcing decisions (addresses G1, G8).**
Require risk input before shortlisting for any sourcing or capital decision that affects the margin–OTIF objective. Apply the same rule to Decision 3 now: the review should shape which proposals reach the committee, not just comment on the final slate.
*How this improves decisions:* risk analysis arrives while options are still open, which is when it changed Decision 1.

**R2. Reassess Decision 2 retrospectively as a live risk position (addresses G1, G6, G7).**
The supplier decision cannot be unmade cheaply, but its exposure can be managed. Answer three questions: Is the second supplier actually qualified? How long would a switch take? Can the consolidated plant absorb a supply interruption of that length while still meeting the OTIF target?
*How this improves decisions:* it turns an unexamined concentration risk into one with known recovery options.

**R3. Separate escalation from the monthly agenda (addresses G2).**
Route trigger breaches *directly* to the action owner (the COO or Head of Procurement) when they occur. Keep the monthly review for confirming actions and learning, not for first notification.
*How this improves decisions:* response time matches the five-business-day commitment, so the boundary works in practice.

**R4. Replace time-limited actions before they expire (addresses G3).**
Before dual-running ends, define the post-dual-running response to a utilization breach. Options might include overtime limits, temporary co-manufacturing, SKU or volume prioritisation, or accelerated capex. Assign the COO as owner.
*How this improves decisions:* every trigger always has a feasible action, so a breach leads to a choice rather than a stall.

**R5. Close the band gaps and align the packaging trigger with appetite (addresses G5).**
- *Utilization:* define what happens between 88% and 90%. Either move the warning to 88% or extend the acceptable range to 90%.
- *Packaging:* either set the trigger at the appetite and assumption limit (+5%), or state explicitly that +5% to +6% is a tolerated band and why. Rename the +4% level a "watch" level, or move it inside the acceptable range deliberately.

*How this improves decisions:* removes ambiguity about who must act and when. Management should set the specific values.

**R6. Extend boundaries to the protective measures (addresses G4).**
Create boundaries for OTIF, complaints and attrition using the same four-part structure. Set OTIF warning and trigger levels *above* 95% so that action starts before the objective is breached.
*How this improves decisions:* stops margin gains being reached by quietly giving up service.

**R7. Add OTIF to the scenarios and run one combined stress case (addresses G4, G7).**
Extend the existing scenario pack; do not create a new one. Add an OTIF outcome per scenario and one combined case: a supplier disruption while utilization is at the downside level. State the expected-case margin.
*How this improves decisions:* shows whether the objective holds under the conditions most likely to break both of its parts.

**R8. Run a short post-event review of July 2026 (addresses G9, G10).**
Answer four questions. Did a trigger fire? Was the +40% assumption reassessed? What did dual-running cost? What is the net value?
*How this improves decisions:* grounds future mitigation choices in evidence and tests whether the trigger and assumption design worked under real stress.
