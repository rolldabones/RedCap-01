## 3. Observations (current state)

**Objectives: Strong.** The objective has a clear target, threshold, population and deadline, and a single accountable owner. It is suitable for risk analysis. Interim milestones and the baseline are unknown. Without a trajectory, deviation cannot be detected early.

**Decision linkage: Limited.** There is no evidence that ERM influenced any decision before options closed. The decisions that most determine success, namely triage design, workforce supply and (likely) bed and capital investment, sit entirely outside ERM.

**Assumptions: Limited.** None are defined or owned. Both 2025 decisions rest on assumptions that are currently unmonitored.

**Uncertainty: Limited.** Uncertainty is expressed only as heat-map scores. There are no scenarios or ranges. Scores convey precision without decision usefulness, which is false precision. Interdependencies are not captured. ED performance typically depends on factors well beyond the ED itself, such as inpatient bed flow and discharge.

**Decision boundaries: Limited.** Risk appetite has not been translated into acceptable ranges, warning thresholds, triggers or required actions.

**Triggers: Limited.** None are defined. The quarterly cadence is structurally mismatched to ED operations, where performance moves weekly. A quarterly report can only confirm a miss after it has happened.

**Operating rhythm: Limited.** ERM operates mainly as a reporting process. It is absent from all seven decision forums listed in the method that apply here.

**Ownership: Limited.** Objective ownership is clear. Ownership of decisions, assumptions, triggers and actions is undefined. Register "risk owners" may exist but are unknown, and register ownership is not the same as decision ownership.

**Value: Insufficient Evidence.** No decision has been improved, no pivot made earlier, no loss avoided and no opportunity enabled that can be attributed to ERM.

## 6. Recommendations (future state)

**R1. Put risk into the monthly performance review for the ED objective.** (Addresses G1, G3)
Add a standing 10–15 minute item covering trajectory versus plan, status of critical assumptions, and any trigger breaches with required actions. The COO chairs, and the risk team provides analysis rather than a register extract.
*Why it improves decisions:* corrective action becomes possible monthly rather than quarterly, inside the forum that already owns delivery.

**R2. Conduct a forward-looking assumption review of the two 2025 decisions.** (Addresses G2)
For each decision, identify the three to five assumptions it depends on, assign an owner, and define what evidence would show the assumption failing. The candidate assumptions below are hypotheses for management to validate, not findings.
- *Triage model:* it reduces time to first assessment; clinical safety is maintained or improved; staff adopt it consistently across shifts; it does not simply move the queue downstream.
- *Agency contract:* the agency can fill requested shifts at the required volume and skill mix; cost stays within budget; agency staff reach productivity quickly; dependency on one supplier is acceptable and exit is feasible.

*Why it improves decisions:* it converts two closed decisions into monitored bets, with an earlier pivot point if they underperform.

**R3. Define a delivery trajectory and decision boundaries for the objective.** (Addresses G3)
Agree interim milestones to June 2027. Then set acceptable, warning and trigger levels relative to that trajectory (see the illustrative register below).
*Why it improves decisions:* it replaces discretionary escalation with pre-agreed action, which speeds response and clarifies accountability.

**R4. Add a short risk check to the transformation program board and capital approval for ED-relevant items.** (Addresses G1, G4)
The check asks four questions, answered on the existing paper rather than a new form:
1. Which objective does this serve?
2. What must be true for it to work?
3. What would make us stop or change course?
4. Who watches for that?

*Why it improves decisions:* risk input arrives before options close, without creating new documentation.

**R5. Reshape the board report around objectives.** (Addresses G5)
Lead with objective trajectory, assumption status, trigger breaches and decisions taken in response. Move the heat map to an appendix or retire it if the board does not use it.
*Why it improves decisions:* the board can challenge delivery and management's responses, not just score movements.

**R6. Build two or three scenarios for the objective.** (Addresses G6)
Examples are a severe winter demand surge, a workforce supply shock, and a downstream bed-flow constraint. For each, agree in advance which actions the organization would take.
*Why it improves decisions:* it prepares responses before pressure arrives and exposes dependencies outside the ED's direct control.
