### Draft Board Pack Insert: Embedding ERM into the Operating Rhythm

*Prepared by management. Original wording, informed by the organization's ERM reference framework.*

**Why it matters.** ERM improves outcomes only when it influences decisions before options close. A risk process running on its own calendar, separate from planning, budgeting and investment cycles, tends to report on decisions already made rather than shape them.

**What embedding looks like in practice:**

| Business cycle | ERM should contribute | Evidence the Board can request |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy setting | Critical assumptions and plausible scenarios for each strategic objective | Strategy papers naming assumptions, owners and the scenarios considered |
| Planning and budgeting | Ranges rather than point estimates, and contingency tied to defined triggers | Budgets showing downside ranges and pre-agreed responses |
| Investment decisions | Decision boundaries derived from risk appetite (acceptable range, warning threshold, trigger, required action) | Investment cases showing where proposals sit against those boundaries |
| Transformation and delivery | Monitoring of the assumptions the programme depends on | Programme reviews that reassess assumptions, not just milestones |
| Executive reviews | Trigger status and actions taken | Instances where a trigger led to a pivot, pause or acceleration |
| Board reporting | Movement in exposure against objectives, and decisions changed | Fewer static heat maps, more "what changed and what we did" |

**Indicators the Board should look for:**
- Named owners for objectives, decisions, assumptions, triggers and actions
- Triggers monitored as part of normal operations, not quarterly risk updates
- Documented examples of ERM changing, accelerating or stopping a decision
- Value enabled (opportunities pursued with confidence) as well as losses avoided

**Questions the Board may ask management:**
1. Which three decisions this year most determine whether our strategic objectives are met, and how did risk input shape them?
2. Which critical assumptions are we monitoring, who owns them, and what happens if they break?
3. Can management cite a decision ERM changed in the past 12 months?
