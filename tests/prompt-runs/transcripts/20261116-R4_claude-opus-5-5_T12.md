## 6. Recommendations

These are future-state recommendations. All thresholds, owners and forums below are proposals for management to set or confirm. None is an existing fact.

**R1. Make risk input a precondition of investment recommendations, not a review after them.**
- Require the investment committee to see the scenario analysis and the assumption confidence levels before a recommendation is finalized.
- Why it improves decisions: it brings uncertainty into the decision while options are still open, which is what happened in partner selection and did not happen in market entry.

**R2. Translate "moderate appetite for expansion" into decision boundaries for each success measure and for capital at risk** (see the Decision Boundary Register below).
- Why: capital allocation and launch timing need a usable limit, not a phrase. Boundaries let management test scenarios against appetite (Gap 3) and make "maintaining profitability and service quality" testable (Gap 11).

**R3. Define a small set of triggers, each with an owner, a monitoring forum and a pre-agreed response.** Start with the regulatory approval timeline and partner performance (see the Trigger Register below).
- Why: this converts the launch-timing decision from an ad hoc judgment into a prepared choice. It shortens the time between a signal and a pivot.

**R4. Assign an owner to each critical assumption, and review assumption status as a standing item in monthly performance reviews.**
- Use one line per assumption: owner, current confidence, leading indicator.
- Why: drift in the two moderate-confidence assumptions is the most direct route to the downside scenario. Named owners make detection someone's job. This uses an existing forum, not a new document.

**R5. Re-run the scenario discussion around actions, not likelihood scores.**
- For each scenario, agree what management would do and at what point.
- Map how an approval delay interacts with partner commitments, capital and margin.
- Why: pre-agreed actions are what make scenarios decision-useful. Interdependency mapping prevents one assumption failing silently into several measures at once.

**R6. Embed ERM in the three decision forums where its presence is inconsistent:** investment committee, transformation governance and monthly performance reviews. Focus on boundary status and triggers, not risk-register updates.
- Why: it moves ERM from reporting what happened to shaping what happens next.

**R7. Hold a short lessons review on the market-entry sequence.**
- If the market-entry decision remains open to adjustment (status Unknown), test its key assumptions against the new boundaries.
- Why: it closes the learning loop and identifies why risk input arrived late, so the pattern does not repeat in later expansion decisions.

**R8. Confirm the remediation status of the cybersecurity integration weaknesses, and make resolution a go-live condition.**
- Why: early identification only creates value if it changes the implementation decision.

## 7. Quick Wins

These can be done within existing forums and without new documentation programs:

- **Name owners** for the three assumptions and the four critical decisions (R4). Owners are a prerequisite for every other improvement.
- **Add a standing item to monthly performance reviews** covering assumption status and the regulatory approval timeline (R4, R6).
- **Agree two triggers with responses:** approval timing (delay and acceleration) and partner milestones (R3). This directly addresses launch timing.
- **Confirm remediation status** of the cybersecurity integration weaknesses (R8).
- **Re-frame the next scenario discussion** around "what we would do" in each case (R5).

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

- **Measurable appetite:** a measurable definition of expansion appetite, cascaded into boundaries for all four success measures and capital (R2).
- **Pre-recommendation risk gate:** a risk gate in the investment process, applied to future expansion decisions, not just this one (R1).
- **Consistent embedding:** ERM embedded consistently in investment committee, transformation governance, planning and budgeting (R6).
- **Board reporting:** a shift toward boundary status, trigger activations and decisions taken, rather than likelihood scores. This lets the board oversee decisions instead of risk lists.
- **Learning loop:** post-decision reviews that record which decisions ERM changed and with what outcome (R7). This builds evidence of ERM value and should be kept to a brief, decision-focused log.
