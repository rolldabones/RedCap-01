## 4. Strengths

- **A well-formed objective.** It is measurable, time-bound and owned. Many organizations lack this anchor. It gives ERM a precise target to work against.
- **Existing executive and board channels.** The quarterly report already reaches the executive committee and the board. Repurposing these channels from describing risks to informing decisions costs less than building new ones.
- **Departmental participation.** Departments already contribute updates each quarter. That habit can be redirected toward monitoring the assumptions behind the objective.

## 5. Gaps

These are current-state facts and their consequences.

1. **ERM arrives after decisions are made.** The triage model and the agency contract were decided without risk input. Material bets on the ED objective were therefore made without structured challenge to their assumptions.
2. **ERM is absent from every forum where resources are allocated.** Planning, budget, capital, transformation and monthly performance reviews have no risk agenda item. Risk therefore cannot shape priorities or funding.
3. **The assumptions behind the objective are invisible.** No one is tasked with knowing whether the bets underpinning the June 2027 target still hold.
4. **There are no usable decision boundaries or triggers.** Scores signal how much concern exists. They do not tell anyone when to act, or what to do.
5. **The cadence is calendar-driven.** A quarterly cycle may detect deterioration up to three months late. The monthly performance review, a faster forum that already exists, receives no risk input.
6. **ERM effort produces no observable decision value.** Resources go into collection and reporting, with no demonstrated effect on outcomes.

## 6. Recommendations

These are future-state recommendations. The registers below are proposed structures. Every threshold marked "To be set" must be calibrated by management from data. None is supplied here.

**R1. Anchor ERM to the ED objective's decisions, not the register.**
- **What:** Make the COO's objective the organizing unit. For each material decision affecting it, record the assumptions, boundaries and triggers before approval.
- **Why it improves decisions:** Risk analysis then reaches decision-makers while options are still open, rather than describing outcomes afterwards.

**R2. Retrospectively surface the assumptions behind the two decisions already made.**
- **What:** Identify the assumptions behind the triage model and the agency contract. Assign each an owner and a monitoring method.
- **Why it improves decisions:** Both commitments are live. Knowing what must stay true allows an earlier pivot if it stops being true.
- **Candidate assumptions (hypotheses for management to confirm, not supplied facts):**
  - The triage model reduces time-to-assessment as intended.
  - The agency contract delivers the required staffing cover reliably.
  - Downstream patient flow, such as bed availability, does not offset ED gains.

**R3. Add risk to the agendas of existing forums.**
- **What:** Add a standing item to the monthly performance review, the transformation program board and capital approval. Include planning and budget at their next cycle.
- **Why it improves decisions:** Risk input reaches the forums that set priorities and money, without new committees or reports.

**R4. Translate appetite into decision boundaries for the ED objective.**
- **What:** Use the Decision Boundary Register below.
- **Why it improves decisions:** It converts "how concerned are we" into "when must we act, and who acts". This shortens reaction time.

**R5. Replace calendar reassessment with event triggers.**
- **What:** Use the Trigger Register below.
- **Why it improves decisions:** Reassessment then happens when conditions change, not when the quarter ends.

**R6. Reshape the board report around the objective.**
- **What:** Report progress against the four-hour target, the status of critical assumptions, any triggers fired and the actions taken. The heat map can become secondary.
- **Why it improves decisions:** The board can oversee whether the objective is on track and whether management is responding. A ranking of risks does not show this.

## 7. Quick Wins

These are achievable within existing forums and need no new documentation layers.

1. **Hold a one-hour assumptions session on the two live decisions** (R2), led by the COO. *Why:* it surfaces the bets the June 2027 target now depends on.
2. **Add an ED objective item to the next monthly performance review** covering progress, the key assumptions and any concerns. *Why:* it gives a monthly rather than quarterly view of the objective.
3. **Add one question to capital approval and transformation board templates:** "Which objective does this affect, what must be true, and what would make us stop?" *Why:* it forces assumptions into view before approval.
4. **Redirect part of the quarterly departmental collection effort** toward monitoring the ED assumptions. *Why:* it reuses existing effort for decision-relevant information.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

1. **Integrate risk into the annual planning and budget cycles.** Objectives and their resourcing would then be set with explicit assumptions and boundaries. *Why:* priorities and funding reflect uncertainty from the start.
2. **Extend the method to other objectives.** Their existence is Unknown. *Why:* it makes ERM an enterprise capability rather than a single-objective pilot.
3. **Develop scenario or range views of the four-hour trajectory** that show the interdependencies between staffing, triage and flow. *Why:* it avoids false precision and shows where the objective is most fragile.
4. **Establish a learning loop.** After each fired trigger or major decision, record what was assumed, what happened and what changed. *Why:* this is how governance improves future decisions and how ERM value becomes demonstrable.
5. **Evaluate whether the heat map still earns its cost** once objective-based reporting is established. *Why:* it frees capacity for decision support.
