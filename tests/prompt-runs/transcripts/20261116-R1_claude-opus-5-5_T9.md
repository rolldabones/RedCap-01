## 6. Recommendations

All items below are future-state recommendations. Named roles are proposals for management to confirm.

**R1. Name a single objective owner for "two aerospace contracts by end 2027."**
- This owner is accountable for the aggregate risk view presented at each bid approval.
- *Decision benefit:* one person integrates functional risks before the CEO decides.

**R2. Define decision ownership for the decisions that determine success, starting with bid approval.**
- Record who prepares (sales), who approves (CEO) and which risk and assumption owners must be consulted before approval.
- *Decision benefit:* risk input arrives while options are still open.

**R3. Assign owners to critical assumptions.**
- The certification timeline assumptions should have a named owner. The Quality Director is a candidate, pending management confirmation.
- Link these assumptions to the "certification delay" risk.
- *Decision benefit:* removes the sales/quality seam and keeps bid commitments credible.

**R4. Replace "monitor" with owned actions linked to triggers.**
- Each action states what the owner does, when, and what decision it informs.
- *Decision benefit:* ownership becomes an obligation to act, not to observe.

**R5. Define trigger owners and embed triggers in existing routines.**
- Use routines such as bid reviews and supplier and certification status meetings rather than new reports.
- *Decision benefit:* reassessment happens in normal operations.

## 7. Quick Wins

1. **Name the objective owner (R1).** No new documentation is needed, and integration of risk input improves immediately.
2. **Add a pre-approval check to CEO bid sign-off.** Confirm that the certification assumptions have been reviewed by the certification delay risk owner. This closes the main ownership seam at the decision point.
3. **Rewrite the "monitor" actions for the risks the objective owner judges most relevant to contract wins.** State the action, the owner and the decision informed. This converts ownership into action without redesigning the register.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

1. **Reorganize ownership around the objective rather than functions.** Map all 25 risks to the objective and retire or deprioritize risks with no material link. Owner attention then concentrates on what determines success.
2. **Set decision boundaries and triggers with management-defined values (R4, R5).** Embed them in bid, supplier and certification routines. This allows earlier pivots before commitments are made.
3. **Record decisions changed by risk input.** Examples include a bid repriced, delayed or declined, or an assumption revised. This provides the evidence needed to demonstrate ERM value and supports board oversight.
4. **Extend ownership to other decisions that determine success,** once management identifies them. Possible examples, to be confirmed: capacity or certification investment, and supplier diversification.

## 9. Management Questions

1. Who is accountable for the objective of winning two aerospace contracts by end of 2027?
2. Besides CEO bid approval, which decisions determine whether the objective is achieved, and who owns each?
3. Do risk owners or the register inform bid approval today? Can you give an example where risk input changed a bid?
4. Who owns the certification timeline assumptions in bid documents? Are they reconciled with the Quality Director's "certification delay" risk?
5. In practice, what does "monitor" involve: who, how often, and reported to which forum?
6. Which of the 25 risks have been assessed as material to the contract objective?
7. Has risk appetite or tolerance been defined for bidding or aerospace growth?
8. Are scenarios or ranges used for certification timing, supplier capacity or skills availability?
9. Where does ERM appear in strategy, planning, budgeting and executive or board reviews?
