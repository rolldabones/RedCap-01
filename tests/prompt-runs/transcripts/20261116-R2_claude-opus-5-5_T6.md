## 7. Quick Wins

1. **Hold one pre-decision session on the fee-model change.** Cover the options, a small set of scenarios for retention outcomes and the acceptance assumption. This gives ERM its first chance to change a decision while it is still open.
2. **Agree how the acceptance assumption will be tested and who will act on the result.** This lowers the cost of being wrong.
3. **Set the retention warning threshold and its required action.** It is one number and one action, and it converts appetite into a usable boundary.
4. **Name the fee-model decision owner.** This clarifies who weighs the risk input.
5. **Lead the next quarterly report with the open decision affecting the 92% objective,** its assumption status and the agreed trigger. This redirects existing reporting effort toward the decision rather than adding new documentation.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

1. **Move risk input upstream.** Require objective-linked risk input before business cases are finalized for client-facing or material investment decisions, so ERM shapes options rather than scoring them.
2. **Extend objective-to-decision-to-assumption-to-trigger mapping to the other enterprise objectives,** once management supplies them.
3. **Translate appetite into boundaries objective by objective,** replacing the single qualitative statement where it does not guide decisions.
4. **Align ERM rhythm with the decision calendar.** Cover planning, budgeting, investment gates and transformation reviews, and keep the quarterly report as a summary rather than the primary channel.
5. **Establish a learning loop.** After major decisions, compare outcomes with assumptions and triggers, starting with the CRM replacement. This lets each decision improve the next.
6. **Track ERM value explicitly.** Record decisions changed, pivots made, opportunities enabled and losses avoided, so the board can judge whether ERM is improving decisions.
