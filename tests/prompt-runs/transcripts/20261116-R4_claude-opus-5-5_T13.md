All items in this section are **future-state recommendations**, not observations. Thresholds, owners and actions marked "to be set" must be supplied by management. None are drawn from the input.

**R1. Bring ERM into the fee-model decision before it closes.**
Ask the decision owner to test the assumption "clients accept the new fee model" with evidence before approval. Examples include client segmentation, pilot results and competitor pricing. Describe the retention outcome as a range across scenarios rather than as a heat-map score.
*Why it improves decisions:* it is the only named decision described as still under review. Risk input can change the option chosen, its design or its rollout sequencing.

**R2. Move risk input upstream of business cases.**
For any decision material to the retention objective, include risk input while options are being framed, not after the case is written.
*Why it improves decisions:* risk then shapes which options are considered, not just how a chosen option is scored.

**R3. Translate "moderate appetite for change" into a retention decision boundary.**
Express appetite as an acceptable range, a warning threshold, a trigger and a required action, anchored to the 92% objective.
*Why it improves decisions:* executives get a usable test for when to proceed, pause or change course.

**R4. Define and embed triggers.**
Assign a monitor and a required action to each trigger. Route triggers through existing commercial performance reviews rather than the quarterly risk report alone.
*Why it improves decisions:* reassessment then happens when conditions change, not only when the calendar allows.

**R5. Surface CRM assumptions and the interdependency with the fee model.**
Identify and assign owners for the assumptions behind the CRM replacement. Assess whether the CRM rollout and the fee-model change could coincide in their effect on clients.
*Why it improves decisions:* it allows sequencing choices, such as staging the fee change around migration, that could protect retention.

**R6. Make decision and action ownership explicit.**
Name the owner of each decision and each mitigating action.
*Why it improves decisions:* accountability for acting on risk input becomes clear.

**R7. Reframe the quarterly report around the objective.**

*Why it improves decisions:* the executive and board discussion becomes "are we on track and what should change", not a review of artifacts.

**R8. Record decision outcomes.**

*Why it improves decisions:* this creates the evidence base for demonstrating ERM value, and the learning loop.
