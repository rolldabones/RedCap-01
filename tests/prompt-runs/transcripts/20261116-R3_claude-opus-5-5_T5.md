## Draft board-pack insert: Embedding ERM into the operating rhythm

*Original drafting by RedCap-01. It does not reproduce or paraphrase specific COSO passages. Everything below is a recommendation, not a finding about your organization.*

**Why it matters**
ERM improves outcomes only when it shapes decisions while options are still open. A risk process that runs alongside management reports on decisions after they are made. A risk process built into management's existing cycles changes those decisions.

**What "embedded" looks like**
- **Strategy:** Objectives are set together with their critical assumptions and the scenarios that would invalidate them.
- **Planning and budgeting:** Resource allocation reflects the agreed decision boundaries: acceptable range, warning threshold, trigger and required action.
- **Investment decisions:** Business cases state the main uncertainties, the assumption owners and the conditions for revisiting the decision.
- **Transformation and delivery:** Programme gates include reassessment against triggers, not just status reporting.
- **Executive reviews:** Discussion centres on objectives at risk, assumptions that have moved and triggers that have been reached.
- **Board reporting:** Directors see which decisions ERM changed, which triggers fired, and what was learned.

**Signals that ERM is still mainly a reporting process**
- Risk reviews follow their own calendar rather than the decision calendar.
- Registers are updated, but no decision record cites them.
- Thresholds exist on paper, with no named owner or defined required action.

**Questions the board may wish to ask**
1. Which significant decisions in the last cycle did risk input change, and when did it arrive?
2. Who owns the assumptions behind our most important objectives, and how often are they tested?
3. Which triggers are monitored within normal operations, and what happened the last time one was reached?
4. Where does risk discussion take place in our strategy, budget and investment cycles?
