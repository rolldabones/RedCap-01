## 7. Quick Wins

1. **Billing migration first.** It is in progress, so exposure may be current (inference).
   - Agree a customer-harm threshold, a named owner and a pause/rollback decision point now.
   - *Why:* It creates a decision point before further migration steps commit customers.
2. **Region-entry go/no-go criteria.** Set criteria tied to regulatory and growth appetite before the Q1 2027 decision.
   - *Why:* Boundaries shape the decision only while options remain open (on assumption A2).
3. **Appetite check in the monthly pricing decision.** Add a short check covering the customer-harm and growth indicators, using existing data if available.
   - *Why:* The decision recurs monthly, so value can be demonstrated quickly.
4. **Board interpretation of terms.** Ask the board to state, in one line each, what "moderate", "low" and "balanced" mean for these three decisions.
   - *Why:* It removes interpretive variance among executives without creating a lengthy document.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

1. **Cascade beyond the three decisions.** Extend boundaries into planning, budgeting and investment approval, so growth spend is tested against appetite before it is approved.
2. **Assumption monitoring.** Assign owners to the growth plan's critical assumptions and review them in executive forums. Re-forecast against ranges rather than point estimates.
3. **Close the coverage gap.** Confirm whether financial, margin and credit risk are governed elsewhere. If not, consider adding them, given their relevance to pricing and growth.
4. **Exception-based board reporting.** Report boundary breaches, triggered actions and decisions changed, rather than static risk lists.
5. **Learning loop.** Review pricing outcomes, the region-entry decision and migration incidents against the boundaries that applied, then recalibrate. This is also how ERM value becomes demonstrable.
