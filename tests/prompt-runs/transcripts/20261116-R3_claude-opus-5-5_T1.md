## 7. Quick Wins

- Write down the objectives each of R1 to R5 threatens or enables, and name an owner for each risk. This can be done in a single executive session.
- Attach to each entry the next scheduled decision it should inform.
- Document what each score level means, so that differences such as 3 versus 4 are interpreted consistently.
- Agree one warning threshold and one trigger for the entry that executives judge most material to objectives.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

- Embed risk discussion in strategy, planning, budgeting, investment and transformation reviews, and at the points where options are still open rather than after they have closed.
- Introduce assumption tracking with named owners for the assumptions that underpin the strategy.
- Use scenarios to examine interdependencies between risks, for example cyber disruption combined with supplier failure during peak trading.
- Establish a learning loop that records decisions ERM changed, pivots taken and outcomes. This is the evidence base for demonstrating the value of ERM.
- Report to the board on objectives, decisions and triggers rather than on the register alone.
