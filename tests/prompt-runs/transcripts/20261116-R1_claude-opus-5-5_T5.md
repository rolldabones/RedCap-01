## Draft board-pack text: Embedding ERM in the operating rhythm

*Prepared by RedCap-01. Not quoted from or attributed to COSO.*

**Why it matters.** ERM adds value only if it reaches decisions while options are still open. A risk process that runs on its own calendar, separate from strategy, planning and investment, tends to produce reports after decisions are already made. The test is whether risk thinking changes what management decides, not whether a register is kept up to date.

**Where ERM should appear.** Risk considerations should be built into the forums where the organization already decides:

- **Strategy setting:** testing critical assumptions and alternative scenarios before choosing a direction.
- **Planning and budgeting:** using risk appetite and decision boundaries when allocating resources.
- **Investment and transformation decisions:** stating ranges, triggers and fallback options at approval.
- **Delivery:** monitoring assumptions and triggers as part of normal performance management.
- **Executive and board reviews:** discussing how uncertainty affects objectives, not only reviewing risk scores.

**What "embedded" looks like.** Each material objective has a named owner. The critical assumptions behind it are known and monitored. Agreed triggers require reassessment and specify a defined action and owner. When a trigger fires, the response happens through normal management channels, not a separate risk exercise.

**What the board should ask:**
- Which recent decisions did ERM change, and when?
- Which assumptions behind our main objectives are being monitored, and by whom?
- What triggers would cause us to revisit our strategy or major investments?
- What have we learned from events where triggers fired or were missed?
