## 6. Recommendations

*Future-state proposals. Role suggestions are recommendations, not facts.*

**R1. Name an objective owner for "two aerospace contracts by end of 2027".**
- Candidates include the CEO or a designated executive sponsor, with management to decide.
- *Why it improves decisions:* someone becomes accountable for the trade-offs across bids, certification, supply and skills, rather than for individual risks in isolation.

**R2. Make risk owners contributors to the bid decision.**
- Before CEO approval, the relevant risk owners (e.g., Quality Director, Head of Procurement, HR Director) confirm or challenge the bid's critical assumptions.
- Record this in the existing bid documents rather than in a new artifact.
- *Why:* ERM input arrives while bid options are still open.

**R3. Assign an owner to each critical bid assumption.**
- The Quality Director is a candidate owner for the certification-timeline assumptions.
- Extend assumption tracking beyond certification only where an assumption is material to winning or delivering.
- *Why:* someone owns the question "is this still true?" between bid submission and award.

**R4. Replace "monitor" with owned responses.**
- For risks linked to the objective, record an action owner, a required action and the condition under which the action is taken.
- *Why:* ownership moves from watching to deciding.

**R5. Define a small set of triggers tied to the objective, each with a named monitor and a required action** (see the Trigger Register below).
- *Why:* triggers make ownership operational and prompt reassessment before bid commitments become hard to reverse.

**R6. Translate appetite into bid decision boundaries** (see the Decision Boundary Register below).
- *Why:* the CEO approves against explicit limits rather than case by case.

**R7. Re-express objective-relevant risks in terms of their effect on the aerospace objective.**

## 7. Quick Wins

1. **Add an "assumption owner" field** next to each certification-timeline assumption already tracked in bid documents. *Why:* it closes the most visible ownership gap at almost no cost.
2. **Add a pre-approval step** in which the Quality Director confirms certification assumptions before the CEO approves a bid. *Why:* it connects the existing risk owner to the decision.
3. **Name the objective owner** and record it once against the objective. *Why:* it creates a single point of accountability.
4. **For the objective-linked risks, replace "monitor"** with one concrete action and its trigger condition. *Why:* it converts register entries into decision-ready responses.

## 8. Longer-Term Improvements

1. **Restructure ownership as a chain:** objective → decisions → assumptions → triggers → actions. Retain risk owners within that chain. *Why:* accountability follows how the objective is actually won.
2. **Agree bid decision boundaries derived from risk appetite.** *Why:* approvals become consistent and defensible.
3. **Introduce simple scenarios** (e.g., certification on time, late or failed) for each target bid, including interactions with supplier and skills risks. *Why:* this avoids false precision and exposes compounding effects.
4. **Embed a bid review in the executive operating rhythm** and report progress on the objective, rather than risk lists, to the board. *Why:* oversight then focuses on whether the objective is being achieved.
5. **Record outcomes,** such as bids changed, withdrawn or strengthened because of risk input, and review them after each bid. *Why:* this demonstrates ERM value and completes the learning loop.
